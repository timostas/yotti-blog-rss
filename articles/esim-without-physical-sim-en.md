Yes. An eSIM can provide cellular service without a physical SIM in the tray. It is a separate digital profile built into a supported device. The empty tray is not proof that service will work, however: the exact phone must support eSIM, accept the provider, be unlocked and complete activation.

## Verify the exact device, not the slot

Apple describes eSIM as a digital SIM that activates a cellular plan without a physical card. Google makes the same distinction for supported Pixel models. Support can still vary by model and sales region. Use the guide to [checking which phones support eSIM](https://yotti.net/en/blog/which-phones-support-esim-how-to-check-before-you-buy) rather than relying on a family name.

If the phone is carrier-locked, a profile from another provider may not work. Keep any working line until lock status and number-transfer arrangements are clear.

## Download the profile on reliable internet

Installation usually needs Wi‑Fi or another connection. Keep the QR code and instructions visible on a second screen. Then follow the [pre-travel eSIM installation order](https://yotti.net/en/blog/how-to-install-an-esim-before-travel-without-starting-it-too-early), including the activation trigger of the exact plan.

## Prove that data moves

After activation, assign the eSIM to mobile data, turn off Wi‑Fi, open two normal pages and send a message. A network icon alone is not a working-data test. If it fails, use the [safe no-network troubleshooting order](https://yotti.net/en/blog/esim-installed-but-no-network-a-safe-troubleshooting-order) and change one setting at a time.

A physical SIM may remain inserted, be disabled or be absent; that is a separate configuration choice. What matters is selecting the correct data line, deciding how the home number will handle calls and SMS, and keeping support details available.
