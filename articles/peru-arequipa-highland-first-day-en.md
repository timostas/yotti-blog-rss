<p class="yotti-deck">Treat arrival in Arequipa as a city day, not as the first leg of a Colca Canyon excursion. Leave your luggage, make one compact circuit of the historic centre and decide after each section whether another is useful.</p>

This need not be an empty day. UNESCO describes the centre through walls, arches, vaults and courtyards made from white and pink volcanic sillar. Those details sit close together and reward a slower pace. A distant mirador will add little if the journey and the elevation are already demanding your attention.

## Let the square set the pace

Plaza de Armas is a useful starting point because its arcades let you alternate short walks with shade. Cross one side, notice how the morning light changes the sillar, and sit down. Wanting to hurry immediately towards the next landmark is a good reason to remove something from the plan.

An official Peru Travel programme connects the square with the cathedral, the Church of the Compañía de Jesús and its cloisters. Pick one interior for an arrival day. Santa Catalina, a museum and a viewpoint each deserve attention of their own; fitting all three between transfers merely turns the centre into a corridor.

## Use a courtyard as an actual pause

Look for somewhere calm to sit rather than the most famous available name. The Compañía cloister works when it is open; a café courtyard or your accommodation lobby can do the same job. Drink water, eat something suitable for you and confirm the route home before adding another stop.

The CDC notes that travellers above 8,000 feet can be at risk of altitude illness. That statement is not a diagnosis and this article cannot tell you what treatment you need. Its useful implication for a city walk is narrow: avoid loading the first hours with strenuous activity, do not argue with symptoms and never continue solely because a ticket has been paid for.

## Add San Lázaro only while the walk feels easy

After the break, San Lázaro offers a different texture without requiring another vehicle. Its small squares and narrow lanes contrast with the ceremonial scale of Plaza de Armas. Take one street out and a different one back; the district remains available tomorrow if your pace starts to feel laboured.

Watch the material rather than searching for a generic colonial backdrop. Thick walls, corners, arches and visible repairs make the place legible. UNESCO explains Arequipa's architecture as an integration of European and Indigenous building traditions shaped by an earthquake-prone setting. That account says more than the usual “White City” nickname.

## Keep the distant decision for tomorrow

Colca, outlying viewpoints and an early intercity departure belong to another day. Before booking one, find breakfast, confirm transport and see how you feel after sleep. Do not make a non-refundable plan on impulse simply because the centre looked small on a map.
