Wi-Fi Calling can work on an eSIM line, provided the carrier supports the service for that number, plan and phone. Installing an eSIM does not establish that support. A travel data package also does not prove that your usual phone number can receive calls over hotel Wi-Fi.

The useful pre-trip test is a call to and from the number you intend to keep using. A successful conversation in a messaging app checks something else: the app has an internet connection. Treat the two results separately when deciding how people should reach you abroad.

## Start with the number people will call

A dual-SIM phone may use one line for your familiar number and another for travel data. Enable Wi-Fi Calling on the line that owns the number you want to use. That may still be a physical SIM; the additional travel eSIM is not automatically the voice line.

Apple's setup instructions explicitly ask people with multiple SIMs to select a line. Check its number before changing the setting. Labels such as Home and Travel make the menu easier to read, but the number identifies which service you are testing.

If a plan is sold as data-only, do not assume it provides ordinary carrier voice calls. Our explanation of [whether an eSIM has a phone number](https://yotti.net/en/blog/does-an-esim-have-a-phone-number) separates the SIM format from the services included in a plan. For a call through the phone's regular dialler, the relevant provider is the carrier of the calling number.

## Get a useful answer from your carrier

Ask whether Wi-Fi Calling is available on your exact line, phone model and plan in the country you are visiting. Ask separately how incoming calls and calls to your intended destinations are charged. Google notes that additional charges may apply; the Wi-Fi connection is not a promise of free calls.

A compatibility answer about eSIM alone does not settle this question. The carrier needs to confirm its Wi-Fi Calling service. Save the response with the plan name and date, especially if receiving calls on your usual number is part of your travel arrangements.

A test at home is worthwhile, but it cannot establish permission or availability abroad. Likewise, a completed call does not test delivery of an SMS verification code. If receiving messages is essential, include that specific service in your question instead of treating all communication as one feature.

## Turn on the service for that line

On iPhone, open Cellular settings, choose the relevant line and open Wi-Fi Calling. Apple says activation may ask you to confirm an emergency-services address. Follow the provider's instructions for that field; do not invent an address to get past it.

Google's Android guidance places the option under Network & internet, SIMs and the carrier SIM. Menus vary between manufacturers. If the switch is absent, consult the carrier and the instructions for your particular phone. Deleting the eSIM is not a way to establish whether the service is supported.

Join a working Wi-Fi network and look for the service indicator. Apple describes Wi-Fi beside the carrier while viewing Control Center. Google describes a Wi-Fi Calling or internet-call indication. The ordinary Wi-Fi symbol only tells you that the phone is connected to a wireless network.

## Test outgoing and incoming calls

Choose a familiar contact after checking the tariff. Note the selected line, whether the carrier's Wi-Fi Calling indicator appears and whether the conversation connects. Ask the contact to call the same number back. An outgoing call alone does not test the reason many travellers keep their home line available: receiving calls.

Repeat the check at your accommodation. Keep the conditions simple enough to explain later. Changing both SIMs, roaming settings and the Wi-Fi network at once makes an unsuccessful result difficult to interpret. A small record of what actually happened is more useful than a long list of attempted fixes.

The result applies to that line on that network at that time. If browser pages load but the carrier indicator never appears, check service availability for the selected line. If websites also fail, restore the Wi-Fi connection first. If a call drops while you move, note whether the network or service indicator changed before retrying.

## If the service still will not connect

Apple includes trying another Wi-Fi network in its troubleshooting guidance: some networks do not work with Wi-Fi Calling. Check the selected line and restart the phone before considering more disruptive steps. A full network-settings reset is a poor first experiment when you depend on saved connection details during a trip; understand its effects on your model before using it.

Give your carrier the phone model, software version, country, selected line and the test results. Use its secure support channel for account identification. A screenshot of a missing indicator can help, but an eSIM QR code is unnecessary for describing this fault. If the actual problem is a missing message, follow the separate [eSIM SMS checks](https://yotti.net/en/blog/sms-not-working-on-esim-what-to-check-first).

Using one SIM's mobile data to support another line's calls is a further device-and-carrier-specific arrangement. This guide tests a Wi-Fi network and does not promise that your home number can use a travel eSIM's data connection. If you need that arrangement, name both lines when asking the carrier. Until it is confirmed, give your contacts a working app-based alternative for reaching you.
