<p class="yotti-deck">Mobile data in Botswana does its most useful work before the safari begins: opening an address in Gaborone, receiving a driver's message, confirming a meeting in Maun and completing every download before the delta. By the time the journey reaches the Okavango, the phone should no longer be the only copy of the plan.</p>

This route cannot be reduced to “coverage” or “no coverage”. In the capital and larger settlements, data handles ordinary city tasks. Beyond them, distances grow and the logistics may move from road to water or a small airstrip. A camp or boat station can sit far from a dependable signal. A well-prepared eSIM supports the journey; it does not replace preparation.

## Buy and install before the flight

Begin with two checks: the phone supports eSIM and it is not locked to one carrier. Then compare the plan's validity and data allowance with the actual trip. A week divided between Gaborone and Maun uses data differently from a safari with occasional connections. A larger allowance cannot help when the device cannot see a network.

Install the profile at home over reliable Wi-Fi. Keep the QR code or manual installation details somewhere other than the phone itself. Installation and the start of validity are not always the same event. Yotti lists the condition for each current product; the Botswana destination page describes validity as beginning when the plan first connects to a local network. Read the selected plan again before purchase.

## Test the complete chain in Gaborone

After arrival, turn off data on the home SIM if you want to avoid accidental roaming and assign the travel line to mobile data. A roaming eSIM generally needs data roaming enabled on that line; follow the instructions for the particular plan. Allow a few minutes for network registration.

Do more than look for a 4G symbol. Open a map, send a message and load a small web page. Check the remaining allowance through the available account or support route. Once the line works in Gaborone, leave the settings alone unless there is a reason to change them.

The Government of Botswana notes that Wi-Fi hotspots are available in major malls and airports. Treat them as a useful fallback rather than the foundation of the journey. Public Wi-Fi may be fine for ordinary search, but banking or document transfer should wait for a trusted connection.

## Treat Maun as the final download point

Maun is the gateway to the Okavango. Finish every task that seemed minor at home: obtain the operator's exact meeting point, confirm a transfer, download boarding documents or vouchers, tell family when to expect contact and charge every device.

A lodge name alone may not be enough for a driver. Save coordinates, the name of the person meeting you, a flight number and the procedure for a delay. When the next leg uses a small aircraft, baggage rules and check-in times come from the carrier or operator, not from a general aviation article.

Open every important file in airplane mode before leaving. A reassuring cloud icon may still represent only a preview. The map must display the relevant area without data, and the operator's number should also be on paper or on a companion's device.

## In the Okavango, the plan outranks the signal

The Okavango Delta is an immense system of seasonally flooded wetlands. Access depends on the particular camp: a journey may continue by road, small aircraft or boat. No general coverage map can promise the same connection at all of those points.

When a signal appears, use it for a brief message or an important update. Do not postpone large downloads or make a video call an essential part of the day. Ask the camp whether guest Wi-Fi exists, when it operates and what it can reasonably support. Staff satellite communications are part of an operation's safety arrangements; they do not turn the wilderness into a visitor workspace.

## Build one offline kit for the whole trip

Save the complete route, accommodation and operator contacts, confirmations, insurance and a passport copy. Write meeting places and times, transport references and the backup contact procedure separately. Download a language in the translation app if useful, plus a map reaching back to the last dependable town rather than covering only the lodge.

Keep a tested cable and power bank in the day bag. Reduce brightness, stop heavy background updates and avoid draining the battery through a constant network search. The phone remains a useful camera and document store offline—provided it still has power in the afternoon.

## If mobile data does not appear

Start with settings that are safe to reverse. Confirm that the eSIM is assigned to mobile data, data roaming is enabled on that line as instructed, and the home SIM has not remained selected. Turn airplane mode on briefly, then allow the device to register again.

If automatic network selection fails, open the network list and use a permitted partner shown for the current plan. Do not invent an APN and do not delete the profile; another installation may not be available. Take screenshots of the line status and settings, note the place and time, and contact support over available Wi-Fi.

No signal at a remote camp does not prove the profile is faulty. Once back in a settlement, let the phone find a network and repeat a small test. Troubleshooting is meaningful where a network is expected, not in the middle of the delta.
