Strong wind in Dunedin shortens exposed transfers and makes the coast a poor automatic choice. Keep the day central: view the railway station from steady ground, spend the main block at Toitū Otago Settlers Museum and finish in a café near the ride home.

## Use the station forecourt as the test

Dunedin's official visitor guide highlights the ornate railway station as a central architectural landmark. Stay clear of the road edge and avoid opening a large umbrella in sharp gusts. Make one short exterior circuit, then decide whether being outside still feels controlled.

The [Porto rainy-day plan](https://yotti.net/en/blog/portugal/a-rainy-day-in-porto-market-tram-and-warm-pauses) relies on awnings. Dunedin instead asks you to avoid wind tunnels between buildings.

## Give the museum real time

DunedinNZ describes Toitū as a social-history museum with themed galleries about people, transport and the city's development. Verify current hours, then choose two themes rather than attempting every room. Leave wet clothing or bags only where the museum permits.

The [hot-day plan in Mendoza](https://yotti.net/en/blog/argentina/mendoza-on-a-hot-day-shade-plazas-and-an-evening-street) also moves the middle of the day indoors, for the opposite weather and clothing problem.

## Return on the protected side

Before leaving, check wind direction, secure loose items and choose the side of the street with steadier shelter. If gusts have strengthened, call transport from inside. Do not add Tunnel Beach or an exposed peninsula stop without a separate conditions check.
