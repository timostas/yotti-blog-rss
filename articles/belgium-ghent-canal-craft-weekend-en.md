<p class="yotti-deck">Spend a Ghent weekend following two threads: water and making. Walk the old quays before they fill, devote the middle of the day to one craft or industrial process, then return through Patershol as the canal lights come on.</p>

Central Ghent is compact enough to encourage over-planning. Towers, museums, churches and famous interiors accumulate quickly, yet the city's character often sits between them: a working shop, a bicycle against brick, metal type in an old mill and a small quay used as an evening seat. Choosing one material gives those fragments a connection.

## Saturday begins at the old harbour

Start between Graslei and Korenlei, cross St Michael's Bridge and let the River Lys establish direction. Visit Gent's official waterside walk continues much farther, through Portus Ganda, the Lieve canal and several districts. For this weekend, the full 8.4-kilometre loop would weaken the day. Shorten it around Waterwijk.

That section moves from open quays to narrow lanes and towards the Museum of Industry. Stop for coffee only once you have chosen the day's craft: leather, print, textiles, jewellery or ceramics. The choice is more useful than a long list of addresses because it changes what you notice in windows and museum displays.

## Follow one maker, not a shopping list

Visit Gent's Made in Ghent directory ranges from leather and jewellery to coffee roasting and fashion. Pick one place where the process is visible, then confirm whether visitors are welcome. A small studio is a workplace, not a museum with guaranteed opening hours.

Ask about the material rather than the price first. How is a hide selected? Which stages belong to the maker? Where is a ceramic piece fired? What separates a hand print from a production run? Seeing one object move from tool to finished form creates a stronger memory than browsing five unrelated souvenir shelves.

## Industry is the useful fallback

When a private studio is closed, the Industriemuseum provides a dependable second route into making. Printing presses and textile machinery place hand skills inside Ghent's larger history of labour and production. The former factory building also fits the Waterwijk walk without a long detour back across the centre.

Choose either print or textiles. In the printing section, follow type, ink and pressure. In textiles, watch fibre become thread and cloth. Demonstrations and access to working machines vary, so consult the museum's current page before building the visit around a particular activity.

## Give Sunday a different starting point

Begin the second day at Portus Ganda, the confluence of the Lys and Scheldt, rather than repeating Graslei. Follow the water towards Patershol. Its medieval street pattern now holds homes, cafés, galleries and restaurants, but it remains a neighbourhood before it is a dining district.

  <img src="https://timostas.github.io/yotti-blog-rss/assets/inline/ghent-patershol-lane.webp" width="1440" height="960" loading="lazy" decoding="async" alt="Residents' bicycles and opening doors along a narrow cobbled lane in Ghent's Patershol district">

Lunch need not prove that you have eaten every Ghent speciality. One local element is enough: bread, mustard, cheese, chocolate or beer from a small city brewery. Coffee and baking serve the same purpose when alcohol is irrelevant. Read the current menu and choose the room as carefully as the dish.

## Finish where the light reaches the canal

From Patershol, the Lieve brings you back towards the central water. Blue hour arrives in reflections before the sky is fully dark. Windows begin to glow, bicycles continue through the frame and the quays become evening rooms for residents and visitors.

Keep the central map and the current hours of your chosen workshop or museum offline. Save the [installation guide](https://yotti.net/en/how-it-works) if the profile will be added before departure.

Water holds the geography together. A single craft provides the question. By Sunday evening, Ghent feels less like a collection of façades and more like a city where old spaces still support ordinary work.
