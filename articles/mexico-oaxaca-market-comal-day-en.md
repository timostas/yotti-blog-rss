Oaxaca's markets make it easy to order more than you can understand in one morning. Begin at Mercado Benito Juárez and choose one product to compare, then move to Mercado 20 de Noviembre for a hot dish. The comal and smoke corridor are distinct scenes; neither needs to become a tasting marathon.

## Look first at Benito Juárez

Oaxaca's official tourism portal places Mercado Benito Juárez in the historic centre, one block from the Zócalo, and describes products connected to different parts of the state. Make one circuit before buying. Dried chiles, cacao, cheese, bread and craft objects each reward a different question.

Ask how a particular chile is used or how the cheese in front of you differs from the next one. Do not turn a guess about origin into the vendor's story. A [market day in Montevideo](https://yotti.net/en/blog/uruguay/a-day-in-montevideo-market-quiet-blocks-and-the-rambla) uses the same observe-first move in a very different food culture.

## Clarify the comal order

A memela or another griddle-cooked base may look small, but several toppings quickly become a heavy meal. Point to the dish, ask about ingredients and heat, and order one portion. If you have an allergy, ask directly; “not spicy” does not answer the same question.

The [batik morning in Yogyakarta](https://yotti.net/en/blog/indonesia/a-batik-morning-in-yogyakarta-workshop-wax-and-a-considered-cloth) reads a process through material and the maker's hands. At the comal, observation matters more than collecting dish names.

## Put water between flavours

Tejate, chocolate and other drinks vary in ingredients and sweetness. Ask what is being served today before ordering. Do not automatically combine an alcohol tasting, a dense breakfast and a hot walk.
