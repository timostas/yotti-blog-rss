There is no reason for an eSIM to receive a weaker radio signal merely because the subscription is digital. An eSIM stores plan credentials inside the device; a physical SIM stores them on a removable card. The network, radio band, phone modem and antennas, location, obstacles and congestion shape reception. A useful comparison therefore changes one variable only.

## Eliminate the network difference first

If the eSIM and physical card use different operators or partner networks, the result says nothing about SIM format. Record the network name, connection type and plan terms. A 5G icon is not a universal score either: [why an eSIM may not connect to 5G](https://yotti.net/en/blog/why-your-esim-is-not-connecting-to-5g) includes network, plan, bands and settings.

## Repeat the test in the same places

The FCC lists spectrum, network load, device capability, indoor or outdoor position, terrain, obstacles and weather among mobile-coverage factors. Move from deep inside a building to a window and then outdoors while keeping other conditions unchanged. One jump in the indicator is not a stable result.

## Separate bars from data performance

Full bars can coexist with slow data. Turn off Wi‑Fi, open the same light page and repeat after a minute. The guide to [finding why a travel eSIM is slow](https://yotti.net/en/blog/travel-esim-running-slowly-how-to-find-the-cause) checks congestion, allowance and background traffic.

If a network is visible but data does not move, that is a different problem. Follow the [signal-but-no-data troubleshooting order](https://yotti.net/en/blog/esim-has-signal-but-no-internet-what-to-check) without deleting the profile.

The practical answer is simple: an eSIM is not penalised by the absence of plastic. Compare network conditions, then record time, network and repeat result if support needs to investigate.
