## What to have ready before arrival

- A pre-installed eSIM is more useful in the medina than trying to solve connectivity after landing.
- Check eSIM compatibility first, then choose data for your actual trip rather than automatically taking the largest plan.
- Keep an offline map, your riad contact, and a meeting point; mobile data should help you navigate, not be the only way to find your accommodation.
- Marrakech’s medina is the historic heart of the city, with narrow lanes, souks, riads, and Jemaa el-Fna nearby, so a little preparation pays off.

## Why the medina makes a good case for an eSIM

The first morning in Marrakech rarely feels like a conventional city checklist. A street can narrow into a passage, a familiar landmark can vanish around a corner, and the entrance to a riad may look much quieter than you expected. That is not a flaw in the place; it is part of its character. Morocco’s official tourism office describes the medina as the city’s historic heart, where markets, crafts, cafés, and traditional courtyard riads sit close together.

Here, connectivity serves ordinary needs: opening a saved route, reading your host’s directions, or sending a message when you are close but cannot see the entrance. Buying an eSIM before the trip is not really about collecting gigabytes. It is about not making a search for Wi-Fi the opening scene of your Marrakech visit.

## Choose an eSIM for the trip you are actually taking

Start with a more useful question than “which plan has the most data?” What will you need on your first day? If the answer is maps, messages, and a riad address, a reliable allowance for navigation matters more than a very large package intended for occasional video. If you expect to send many photos, work on the move, or share a connection with another device, choose more accordingly.

The [Yotti Morocco eSIM page](https://yotti.net/en/esim/morocco) lets you select a plan by data allowance and validity. Before paying, check the current plan terms and make sure your phone supports eSIM. An eSIM provides mobile data; ordinary calls and SMS may not be included, so keep essential contacts both in a messenger and in a note on your phone.

## Set up your phone before the flight

The best time to install an eSIM is a quiet evening at home, not a queue after a flight. After purchase, Yotti sends the installation details. Add the eSIM following the instructions, give it a clear label such as “Morocco”, and do not remove the profile unless you need to. Then check that your regular line remains selected for calls and verification codes, while the new eSIM is used for mobile data.

Make this short pre-flight list:

- save the riad address exactly as it appears in the booking, plus a readable version for yourself;
- download an offline map of the area and save any pin your host sends;
- put the host’s number and message thread somewhere easy to reach;
- keep the booking reference outside your email inbox;
- enable the new eSIM for data only when you are ready to use it.

This takes little time, but it leaves room for a pause at a juice stall, a look into a lantern shop, or one wrong turn without the feeling that the whole arrival has gone off course.

## Let connectivity help, not distract, on day one

Think of the route to your riad in two parts. The first gets you to a clear nearby point. The second is the final stretch inside the medina. For that second part, do not rely only on a moving blue dot: compare your host’s message, entrances, and real-world landmarks. The medina rewards attention better than haste.

Once you have checked in, resist the urge to “complete Marrakech” immediately. Choose one small loop: walk towards Jemaa el-Fna, notice the way back, and find a quiet café. The square is an important part of city life and changes pace as evening arrives, but it does not need to be the finish line for every first day. Good connectivity gives you the freedom to change your mind: return to the riad, explore another lane, or confirm a meeting without anxiety.

## How much mobile data do you need?

For a week of maps, messages, and occasional photos, you may need less than you imagine before travelling. Automatic photo backup, app updates, and video consume far more data than a map. Turn off mobile updates, download key maps in advance, and use your accommodation’s Wi-Fi for any photo backup.

If you need internet for work, video calls, or a laptop hotspot, do not try to predict usage perfectly. Pick a validity period that suits the trip and check whether top-ups are available under the current plan terms. The point is not to squeeze every megabyte; it is to have maps when they matter and avoid paying for data you will not use.

Prepare the profile and offline details before you fly, then use your phone as a helpful tool rather than a reason to look away from Marrakech.

## Setup and navigation questions

### Can I buy an eSIM for Morocco after landing?

Yes, if you have internet access. Setting it up before departure simply removes several tasks at the moment you are trying to reach your accommodation in a new city.

### Do I need a physical SIM as well as an eSIM?

Not necessarily. If your phone supports eSIM, the digital profile can handle data while your primary SIM stays active for calls and codes. Check the capabilities of your specific device.

### What if the map does not show my riad entrance?

Open your host’s message and compare the meeting point with a nearby clear landmark. A saved address and contact make this easier even if location accuracy is imperfect.

### When should I switch on the eSIM?

Installing it before departure is convenient. Turn on the selected profile for mobile data when you arrive, following the current instructions for the plan you chose.
