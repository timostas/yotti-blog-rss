Many recent iPhones, Google Pixel phones and Samsung Galaxy models support eSIM. The family name alone is not enough to confirm compatibility. Regional editions of the same phone can differ, older devices may have carrier-specific restrictions, and a handset can remain locked to the network that sold it. Check the exact model number, its sales region and the presence of an EID before buying a travel plan.

An eSIM QR code cannot add hardware that the phone does not contain. Confirm the device first, then use [Yotti](https://yotti.net/en/) to choose a plan for the destination.

## iPhone compatibility has regional exceptions

Apple lists iPhone XS, XS Max, XR and later models as eSIM-capable. Depending on the model, an iPhone may combine a physical SIM with an eSIM or keep two eSIMs active. Regional rules still matter. Models intended for mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao have exceptions that cannot be inferred from a name such as “iPhone 15”.

Open Settings, General and About. An EID in this screen indicates that the phone contains an eSIM component. The same page shows Carrier Lock status. A travel eSIM from a different provider normally requires an unlocked iPhone.

Next, look for Add eSIM under Cellular or Mobile Data. If the EID is missing and the add command does not appear after a system update, repeatedly scanning a QR code will not help. Confirm the model number and region with Apple or the seller.

## Google Pixel support depends on generation and origin

Google says that Pixel 4 and later phones support eSIM. Pixel 3, 3a and Pixel 2 have market and carrier exceptions, so an older handset needs a model-specific check.

On a compatible current Pixel, the route normally runs through Settings, Network & internet, SIMs and Add SIM. Menu wording can shift between Android releases. Seeing the setup option confirms that the phone can handle a digital profile, although the carrier must still accept the device and plan.

Using two eSIM profiles at once is a separate capability. Google identifies Pixel 7 and later for this feature and notes that carrier support is also required. A phone may store several profiles while allowing fewer active lines.

## Samsung Galaxy and other Android devices

Samsung compatibility varies by exact Galaxy variant. A supported phone usually shows Add eSIM under Settings, Connections and SIM manager. If that command is absent, possible reasons include the regional edition, software version or carrier configuration.

For Motorola, Xiaomi, Honor and other manufacturers, use the official specification for the complete model number. Retailer lists are useful for a first pass, but they can lag behind software changes and omit regional exclusions.

## A four-part check before payment

Find the model number in the phone settings rather than relying on the box. Then verify:

1. The manufacturer lists eSIM for that exact variant.
2. The phone displays an EID or an Add eSIM command.
3. It is unlocked if the travel plan uses another network.
4. Its operating system is up to date within the device's supported range.

If one answer remains unclear, pause the purchase. Support will usually need the model, purchase region, software version and a screenshot of the SIM settings. Do not post the full IMEI or EID publicly; both identify the device.

## What compatibility does not guarantee

A compatible phone is only the first requirement. The plan must include the destination, the handset needs suitable local radio bands, and downloading the profile requires an internet connection. Some activations also depend on the provider.

If Add eSIM is present but a QR code fails, the question has moved beyond basic compatibility. The code may already be used, the connection may be unstable, or the provider may need to issue new activation details.
