Choose a travel eSIM by matching a plan to the full route and the days when you need service. Then check likely data use, phone compatibility, activation rules and whether the plan includes anything beyond mobile internet. This sequence prevents a generous-looking bundle from failing at a border or expiring before the flight home.

The [Yotti catalogue](https://yotti.net/catalog) lets you begin with a destination and compare the plans currently offered for it. Keep a copy of the selected plan page when you buy; the wording attached to that order matters more than a general description of eSIM technology.

## Map every place where you expect a connection

A country plan is the simplest fit for a one-country itinerary. A regional eSIM may be easier for a rail trip or cruise with several stops, provided every destination appears in its country list. Include long layovers if you expect to use data there.

Do not rely on a regional label alone. “Europe”, “Asia” and “Global” can describe different country sets across providers. Ferries and remote islands raise a separate coverage question: a country being included does not promise signal at sea or in every rural area.

## Count service days, not hotel nights

Write down the first day you will connect and the time of the return journey. Seven nights can require service during parts of eight days. Check whether validity begins when the profile is installed, when it first reaches a supported network, or at another event named by the provider.

Apple's travel guidance distinguishes adding an eSIM from choosing and using it abroad. The handset controls the line, while the provider controls the commercial validity. An “On” switch in Settings therefore cannot tell you how many plan days remain.

## Build an allowance from actual habits

Maps, messages and tickets use a very different amount of data from streaming video, video meetings, photo backups or laptop tethering. Look at a normal week in the phone's mobile-data statistics and remove activities that will stay on hotel Wi-Fi. Add a modest margin for navigation and disrupted Wi-Fi rather than choosing the largest number on the page.

A smaller fixed bundle may suit a traveller who postpones uploads until Wi-Fi. Remote work needs another calculation because a laptop can download updates or sync folders in the background. Confirm that the plan allows hotspot use before relying on it for work.

## Decide where calls and verification texts will live

Many travel eSIMs are data-only. They do not issue a regular phone number, so a bank code addressed to the home number still needs the home SIM. Internet calling and messaging apps can use the travel data connection, though they do not create carrier voice or SMS service.

If a local number is essential for deliveries or ordinary calls, look for explicit voice and SMS terms. The word “SIM” does not establish that these services are included.

## Confirm the exact handset variant

Compatibility can change with the market version of a phone and its carrier-lock status. On an iPhone, check Settings → General → About for an EID and the Carrier Lock entry. On Android, look for an option to add an eSIM, then verify the precise model with the manufacturer's documentation.

An incompatible phone cannot be made compatible by scanning a QR code. Avoid testing the code on another handset because activation credentials may be single-use. Obtain a clear compatibility answer before payment if the device's origin or model suffix is uncertain.

## Keep the five plan answers with the order

Before paying, make sure one record shows the country list, data allowance, validity, activation event and hotspot terms. Keep Yotti's [installation guide](https://yotti.net/how-it-works) nearby so you know which actions happen before departure and which wait until arrival.

Missing information is a reason to ask a specific question or choose another plan. Save the answer with the order confirmation. If service later behaves differently from the plan page, the support team can work from an exact product and a dated set of terms instead of a remembered headline.
