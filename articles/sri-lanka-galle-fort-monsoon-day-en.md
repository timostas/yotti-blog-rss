Rain does not erase Galle’s old town, but it changes the unit of planning. Replace one long rampart walk with short movements between verandas, courtyards and indoor anchors. The sea wall becomes a weather-dependent extra, not a promise the day has to keep.

## Read the sky before the map

Begin inside the street grid. UNESCO highlights narrow streets, houses with deep overhanging roofs, verandas and internal gardens—features shaped by local conditions rather than accidental shelter. Choose a cafe or small museum as the first anchor and let the first squall pass.

## Walk one block, then reassess

When the rain eases, make a small rectangle through the interior lanes and return under cover. Short loops reveal columns, facades and drainage without committing you to an exposed edge. Wet paving and thresholds can be slick, so tread matters more than itinerary density.

## Earn the ramparts with a stable window

The old town occupies a rocky promontory, and its bastions follow that shape. The result is dramatic and exposed. Step onto the walls only with good visibility, manageable wind and no sheet of water crossing the path. A photograph is never a reason to approach the outer edge.
