A useful craft day in Kigali is measured by one genuine conversation, not the number of shopping bags. Visit a workshop or cultural venue in the morning, leave the market for midday and finish at a small gallery or nearby café. An object becomes more legible when you understand its material, labour and maker.

## Watch before choosing

Visit Rwanda presents Kigali as a green city spread across hills and identifies Kigali Cultural Village as a setting for local artisans and food. Use that as orientation, not as a promise of today's programme; confirm opening hours and the kind of visit before travelling across town.

Do not open with a discount request. Ask what the material is, who developed the pattern and how the object should be stored. Ask permission before photographing a person or workspace. The [batik morning in Yogyakarta](https://yotti.net/en/blog/a-craft-morning-in-yogyakarta-batik-and-a-conversation-with-a-maker) offers another maker-first visit, with a different material and pace.

## Compare construction at the market

Look at several examples of the same object. Check the density of the weave, the finish at the rim and whether the form sits steadily. Price matters, but it does not explain quality by itself. If a seller gives a fixed price, do not turn bargaining into a performance of authenticity.

Consider the journey home before buying a large or fragile piece: edges, rigid dimensions and cabin-bag space. Delaying a purchase is better than carrying an awkward object through every later stop. A [craft day in Dakar](https://yotti.net/en/blog/a-craft-day-in-dakar-gallery-market-and-workshop) also starts with observation, though its materials and urban context are distinct. A [Ghent weekend](https://yotti.net/en/blog/belgium/a-ghent-weekend-of-canals-craft-and-patershol) shows another way to connect making with a particular urban district.

## Let a gallery widen the frame

A small gallery or cultural venue after the market can show how familiar forms move between household use and contemporary art. Do not assign a symbolic meaning to a motif unless the maker or a dependable source explains it. An honest unanswered question is better than an invented story.

Write down the maker or cooperative, material and care advice before the day ends. Those details give one chosen object a durable context. The best result is modest: one object, one conversation and a clearer sense of why the work takes its particular form.
