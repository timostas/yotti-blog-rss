Rain in Porto should change the length of the transfers, not trigger a frantic indoor checklist. Give the day three dry anchors: Bolhão Market in the morning, one transport decision after lunch and a café or gallery near the route home. The space between them can expand or disappear with the weather.

## Let Bolhão hold the morning

Mercado do Bolhão sits in the centre and offers a useful first look at everyday food shopping without a long walk over wet slopes. Circle the stalls before ordering. Notice the seasonal produce, available seats and pace, then choose one small breakfast rather than treating the market as a tasting challenge.

If the aisles feel crowded, step under a nearby awning and return. The [market-to-lunch approach in Lima](https://yotti.net/en/blog/what-to-eat-in-lima-a-market-and-peruvian-lunch) uses the same practical move: observe first, order second.

## Treat the tram as a choice

A heritage tram can be a pleasing rainy interlude, but current service and queues need a same-day check. Do not make a particular departure the spine of the day. If the wait is long, take ordinary public transport and save attention for a street you actually want to see.

## Choose one sheltered quarter

After lunch, select a compact area with arcades, shops or galleries. Wet granite can be slippery, so steady footwear and short crossings matter more than a perfect map line. Do not automatically add the riverfront when wind is pushing rain off the water.

The [Bologna portico plan](https://yotti.net/en/blog/bologna-in-the-rain-porticoes-and-warm-pauses) offers another sheltered-city strategy. Porto adds steep streets and Atlantic wind, so mark two places where the route can end without a complicated return.

## Finish close to the ride home

Choose the last café near a straightforward transport connection. A window seat gives clothes time to dry and lets you decide whether another block would still be enjoyable. If rain eases, add it. If the downpour strengthens, stop without treating the shorter route as a failed day.

The same weather-led discipline shapes a [windy day in Wellington](https://yotti.net/en/blog/a-windy-day-in-wellington-museum-coffee-and-short-transfers). Porto's satisfying wet-weather sequence is smaller: a market, one transport choice and a few streets whose stone and light are unusually vivid in rain.
