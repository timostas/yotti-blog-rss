Galway works well as a single base for four days when you want the city, Connemara and one Aran island without moving accommodation each night. Keep the first day in Galway, use two days for contrasting outings, and protect the fourth as a weather buffer. The two excursions can change places when the Atlantic forecast changes.

This is a practical car-free plan, not an attempt to collect the entire west coast. You return to streets you know every evening and avoid carrying luggage through rural connections. The trade-off is reliance on coaches, buses and ferries, so every outing needs a confirmed way home and an offline copy of the details.

## Four days without changing hotels

Day one is for Galway and basic orientation. Day two can run through Connemara; day three can go to the islands. Treat that sequence as provisional. Give the sea outing the clearest forecast and keep the road day for mixed cloud.

Before booking, decide which outing matters most. If Dún Aonghasa and the limestone landscape are central to the trip, protect the best day for an island. If open moorland and Connemara villages are the priority, the ferry can remain optional.

Check live departures and connections with Transport for Ireland and the ferry operator shortly before travel. Route numbers and exact times are deliberately absent here: they change, while the four-day structure remains useful.

## Day one: Galway

The first afternoon does not need an ambitious circuit. Walk from Eyre Square towards Shop Street, then continue to Spanish Arch and the River Corrib. That line connects the transport centre, the compact old streets and the water. It also reveals how long you need to reach the next morning’s departure point.

Galway is better understood as a place to return to than a checklist. Street musicians gather around the centre, pubs change character as the evening develops, and another shower can arrive before the pavement has dried. Rather than reserve one tightly timed music venue, note several possibilities close together and see what is actually happening.

Persistent rain does not waste the day. Shorten the waterfront section, visit Galway City Museum, stop for lunch near the market and return outside when the shower eases. Those same choices can fill the final day if an excursion has already taken place.

## Day two: Connemara

A car-free Connemara day needs restraint. Clifden provides a clear town stop; Letterfrack gives access to marked routes in Connemara National Park. Choose a guided outing or one workable public-transport direction instead of trying to stitch several peninsulas together.

The main decision is how much of the day belongs to walking. Stable conditions allow one of the marked Letterfrack trails. Strong wind makes a shorter route and more time in Clifden the better choice. Neither version needs to be presented as a failed substitute for the other.

Confirm the final stop and return before setting out. In a rural area, a later service may mean a long wait rather than a quick alternative. Keep a screenshot of the journey and the stop name so the plan does not disappear with reception.

## Day three: one Aran island

An island day starts with the full chain: the connection to Rossaveel, the check-in buffer, the sailing and the final trip back to Galway. The operator publishes current arrangements; review them the evening before and again before leaving the hotel.

For a first visit, choose one island. Inis Mór offers the broadest range and Dún Aonghasa, but you must budget the trip from Kilronan and protect the return sailing. Inis Oírr is more compact and suits a slower day among stone walls, village lanes and the shore.

Pick one way to move around after arrival: walking, cycling or a local tour. Combining all three can consume the day in handovers and backtracking. Carry water, a warm layer and rain protection even when the mainland sky looked settled.

The ferry should not hold the entire holiday hostage. If conditions cancel the sailing, use the Galway plan and move the island to day four. If it cannot be moved, accept a city or coast day rather than buying a complicated set of replacement connections.

## Day four: a buffer with a real job

The spare day can absorb the ferry, support a shorter return to Connemara or remain in Galway. A walk towards Salthill gives another view of the bay without creating a new transport project. A long breakfast and an unhurried final evening are valid uses of the time.

If both outings happened as planned, resist adding another distant landmark merely because the calendar has space. A bookshop, the Corrib, a neighbourhood lunch and music after dark provide the local texture that disappears from a coach window.

## What to arrange before departure

Book one Galway stay for all four nights and map the walk to each departure point. A flexible ferry fare can be useful when the island is non-negotiable. A Connemara outing should match your tolerance for connections and your preferred amount of walking.

Keep these offline:

- the accommodation address and route from the station;
- transport confirmations and change conditions;
- maps of Galway, Clifden, Letterfrack and the chosen island;
- ferry contact details and the deadline for changes;
- one wet-weather city plan.

Mobile data helps with service updates and messages to accommodation, but it cannot replace an offline route on the coast. Four days from Galway produce two very different journeys: a road through bog and mountain, then a sea crossing to limestone fields. One base keeps both within sensible limits, while the buffer prevents a single Atlantic forecast from deciding the entire trip.
