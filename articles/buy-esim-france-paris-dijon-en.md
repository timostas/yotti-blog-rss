Buy an eSIM for France before you fly if your trip continues beyond Paris. Install the profile on reliable Wi-Fi, make sure it appears in your phone settings, then select the new line for mobile data after landing. That gives you a working connection for the airport transfer, the right Paris station and the walk to your accommodation in Dijon.

The connection matters most during the awkward transitions: the ten minutes after leaving a station, when you need the correct exit, a saved booking and a message to your host. Once those details are settled, a smaller city is easy to explore without watching a screen.

## Set up the eSIM before departure

An airport is a poor place to install a new mobile profile. You may be switching between an email containing the QR code and the settings screen while trying to collect luggage or find transport. Home Wi-Fi gives you time to read each step, save the instructions and check that the profile has been added successfully.

The [Yotti eSIM page for France](https://yotti.net/en/esim/france) lists the current options and includes a device compatibility check. Use the exact model and sales region of the phone rather than relying on the product name alone. Once the profile is installed, give both lines clear labels, such as “Home” and “France”, and avoid deleting the new one after a test. Installation codes are generally intended for a single device.

The France line can remain switched off until arrival. After landing, turn it on, select it for mobile data and follow the plan instructions for data roaming. A physical or second eSIM can stay in the phone, but it should not remain the accidental source of mobile data.

## Choose data for the way you travel

Adding Dijon to a Paris trip does not automatically require a large allowance. Maps, messages, tickets and accommodation details are relatively light uses. Video, hotspot sharing, automatic photo backup and app updates can consume far more than the journey itself.

Start with the total length of the trip, including arrival day and the final trip back to the airport. Then consider what will happen away from hotel Wi-Fi. If maps are downloaded and entertainment is stored before a travel day, mobile data can be reserved for navigation, communication and changes that genuinely need a live connection.

Photo backup is worth checking before departure. It may start whenever the phone is charging or connected to a fast network, quietly using the allowance while you are on a train. The same applies to operating-system updates and high-quality video. Restricting those tasks to Wi-Fi makes the remaining data much easier to understand.

If the route continues beyond France, read the coverage list for the chosen plan. A country plan should not be assumed to work across an international border simply because the journey remains within Europe.

## Use the airport as the first checkpoint

Turn on the new line while airport Wi-Fi is still available as a fallback. Open a map and send one message. This short test confirms that the phone is using the intended profile before you begin navigating across Paris.

Save the name of the Paris departure station and the full address of the first night’s accommodation. Paris has several major terminals, and similar-looking names are easy to skim when tired. A ticket stored in a wallet app is useful, but a separate screenshot or downloaded file is a sensible backup if the app asks you to sign in again.

For a short stay in Paris, mobile data helps with ordinary transitions: finding the correct street after leaving the Métro, messaging a hotel or opening a reservation. It does not need to dictate the walk. Mark a few fixed points before leaving and use the map only when the street pattern becomes unclear.

## What to save before the train to Dijon

A working connection cannot prevent every awkward app moment. A ticket may need to refresh, an account may request a password, or reception may briefly dip in a crowded station. Before leaving for the platform, keep these essentials available offline:

- the ticket with a readable QR code or barcode;
- the full departure-station name and train number;
- the Dijon accommodation address and check-in instructions;
- a downloaded map between the station and the accommodation;
- the host’s phone number or message thread;
- any onward bookings for the next part of the Burgundy trip.

Platform information and operational changes are live details, so station displays still matter. A screenshot is not a substitute for checking them. Its job is more modest: preserving the basic journey when an app refuses to load at the wrong moment.

The train itself can be an offline interval. Open the Dijon map, mark the accommodation and choose one first walk before departure. Arriving with the neighbourhood already downloaded feels very different from standing on the platform waiting for the map tiles to appear.

## Dijon works best once the phone goes away

Dijon is a useful counterpoint to Paris because its centre is manageable on foot. The city’s official tourism office describes a largely pedestrian historic district with half-timbered houses, churches and private mansions from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The scale encourages wandering rather than a complicated chain of journeys.

Mobile data is most useful during the first part of the arrival: choose the correct exit, find the accommodation and send the check-in message. After that, one clear walking line is enough. The marked Owl Trail passes through the old streets around the Palace of the Dukes of Burgundy, so there are signs to follow when you would rather keep the phone in a pocket.

After check-in, the connection can be left for occasional questions: a restaurant address, a short translation or a place to save for tomorrow. There is no need to reopen a “must-see” list at every corner. Much of Dijon’s appeal lies between planned stops: a market hall, a bakery or a courtyard noticed on the way somewhere else.

## Continuing into Burgundy

Regional days need a little more preparation than a walk through central Dijon. Before travelling to Beaune, a vineyard area or a smaller settlement, save the exact address, the return boarding point and one recognisable landmark. Keep the relevant booking outside an app that depends on a fresh login.

Keep the route itself available offline and use mobile data for changes. If reception weakens, you still know where to get off and how to return. When it comes back, messages and the next address are ready to check.

## If mobile data does not appear

Avoid changing several settings at once. First confirm that the France line is switched on, selected for mobile data and configured for data roaming as instructed. Give the phone time to register on a network, then restart it if necessary.

If there is still no connection, use trusted Wi-Fi to open the saved setup instructions. Deleting the profile should not be the first response, because the same installation code may not work again. A calm check in a fixed order is more useful than a rapid series of unrelated toggles.

By the time you leave the airport, three things are enough: a working data line, an offline ticket and the first accommodation address. With those in place, you can cross Paris, reach Dijon and deal with the remaining details after you have checked in.
