# Competitor-informed publication scorecard

Use this scorecard after the human editorial pass and before publication. It is
a decision aid, not a claim about a search ranking factor. Google does not
recommend a preferred word count, so never add filler to improve a metric.

## Hard gates

Stop publication regardless of score when any item is true:

- the page is a country or keyword substitution of an existing article;
- the title is unnatural, inflated, translated, or promises more than the body;
- a volatile claim lacks a current source and review date;
- a comparison calls Yotti the best without a disclosed, reproducible method;
- RU and EN mirror one another sentence by sentence;
- the article uses a universal takeaways, FAQ, conclusion, or airport-scene
  template that the material does not need;
- links, metadata, category, canonical identity, image, or alt are wrong;
- the site or source shows spam, compromise, deceptive links, or another trust
  failure;
- the article competes with an existing Yotti URL for the same primary intent
  without an explicit update-or-new-URL decision.

## Weighted review

Score the actual page, not the effort that produced it.

### 1. Intent fit — 15

- 13–15: answers the exact reader decision early and fulfils the title;
- 10–12: useful answer, but delayed or partially diluted;
- 0–9: generic background, query repetition, or an unfulfilled promise.

### 2. Practical utility — 15

- 13–15: the reader can act and understands limits, exceptions, and failure
  cases;
- 10–12: useful steps but important choices remain vague;
- 0–9: mostly explanation, inspiration, or product promotion.

### 3. Local specificity and original value — 15

- 13–15: selective local detail, an original scenario, observation, synthesis,
  or calculation that cannot be moved to another country;
- 10–12: several real local details, but the organising idea is familiar;
- 0–9: landmarks decorate an interchangeable connectivity article.

### 4. Trust and evidence — 15

- 13–15: responsible author/reviewer, current dates, primary or authoritative
  sources, clear uncertainty, and no unsupported precision;
- 10–12: generally credible with minor gaps;
- 0–9: unattributed claims, stale facts, cosmetic date changes, or a seller's
  unsupported comparison presented as independent advice.

### 5. Human editorial voice — 15

- 13–15: natural title and headings, concrete language, varied rhythm, clear
  editorial selection, idiomatic locale;
- 10–12: readable but occasionally formulaic or over-polished;
- 0–9: translated, mechanical, promotional, or recognisably GPT-shaped.

### 6. Structure and readability — 10

- 9–10: structure follows this material; lists, tables, and FAQ each perform a
  necessary job;
- 7–8: clear and scannable with small repetitions;
- 0–6: universal template, overlong sections, or headings written for mood.

### 7. Technical publication quality — 10

- 9–10: permanent identity, title and description, author/dates, working links,
  useful image and alt, correct category, clean RSS and public page;
- 7–8: sound publication with a small metadata or presentation weakness;
- 0–6: broken, inconsistent, misleading, inaccessible, or technically unsafe.

### 8. Commercial restraint — 5

- 5: Yotti appears only where it is the useful next step and limitations are
  explicit;
- 3–4: visible promotion, but the article remains independently useful;
- 0–2: the answer is bent toward the product or competitors are framed unfairly.

## Decision

- 85–100: strong publication; release after deterministic checks;
- 82–84: publishable Yotti standard;
- 76–81: use the one allowed repair, then rescore;
- 75 or lower: stop this article and report the weakest criteria.

In addition to 82 total, require these floors: intent 12/15, utility 12/15,
specificity 11/15, trust 10/15, voice 11/15, structure 7/10, technical 8/10,
commercial restraint 3/5. A high total cannot hide a weak essential dimension.

Record the total, the two weakest dimensions, and the change made during the
editorial pass in the content brief or queue quality notes. Do not fabricate a
score after publication.

## Length budget

Choose the range from the reader's task. These are planning ranges per locale,
not SEO targets:

- news or short update: 600–1,000 words;
- focused practical instruction: 900–1,400;
- travel-connectivity scenario: 1,000–1,600;
- route, culture, or food feature: 1,200–1,800;
- comparison or complex technical guide: 1,400–2,200.

For an ordinary evergreen page below 900 words, state in the brief why the task
is fully answered. Above 1,800 words, check every section for a distinct reader
job and remove repeated conclusions. Never lengthen RU to match EN or vice
versa.

## Patterns worth borrowing

- Saily: visible authors, meaningful verticals, research and update discipline;
- Airalo and Holafly: direct service explanations and readable navigation;
- Drimsim's best travel features: a real route, firsthand selection, original
  photographs, costs and observed limitations;
- aloSIM and Nomad: a clear travel or device scenario;
- Ubigi: comparison and troubleshooting depth.

Borrow the editorial function, never the wording, heading sequence, scenario,
or page template.

## Market patterns to reject

- a destination inventory made by replacing names in one page;
- a staged airport lead on every connectivity article;
- repeated “best eSIM” claims by the seller itself;
- exact speed, coverage, price, or activation-time promises without evidence;
- mandatory takeaways, conclusion, and FAQ;
- high publishing frequency that creates near-duplicate event clusters;
- translated Russian and sentence-by-sentence bilingual symmetry;
- generic metaphors, exaggerated enthusiasm, emoji-led arguments, and polished
  closing morals;
- product CTAs inserted before the reader has received a useful answer.

## Post-publication evidence

At 14, 30, and 90 days, use available evidence rather than a generic SEO score:

- indexing status;
- Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR, queries, and page trends;
- non-brand query fit and possible cannibalisation;
- engaged sessions and average engagement time when analytics is available;
- useful click-through to the relevant Yotti page;
- factual or stylistic corrections required.

Build separate baselines for RU, EN, and content format. Compare a page with its
cohort median after enough data exists; do not invent universal CTR or time
targets.
